The article

Your kids don't need a perfect parent. They need a present one.

Last Tuesday, my kids had chicken nuggets for dinner. Again. The laundry was still in the dryer from Sunday. And I spent twenty minutes hiding in the bathroom because I just needed everyone to stop talking at me for one minute.

If you're waiting for me to say "but then I pulled myself together and made a Pinterest-worthy taco bar," keep waiting. We had nuggets. Everyone survived.

Here's what I wish someone had told me when my oldest was born: the bar for "good parent" is so much lower than Instagram makes it look.

What "good enough" actually means

The term comes from pediatrician Donald Winnicott, who coined "the good enough mother" back in the 1950s. His point wasn't to excuse neglect. It was the opposite: kids don't need perfection. They need a caregiver who shows up, messes up sometimes, and repairs it.

Research backs him up. Studies on attachment show that what matters isn't getting it right every time — it's the repair after getting it wrong. The apology after you snap. The hug after the bad morning. That's what builds trust.

Three things to stop feeling guilty about

  1. Screen time. An hour of PBS while you drink your coffee in peace is not ruining anyone. The guilt is worse for you than the show is for them.
  2. Not loving every minute. Parenting a toddler through a grocery store meltdown is not enjoyable. You're allowed to say so. Loving your kids and liking every moment are different things.
  3. Taking time for yourself. The bathroom hide isn't selfish. It's regulation. You can't pour from an empty cup, and you definitely can't parent from one.

The bottom line

Your kids won't remember whether dinner was homemade or heated up. They'll remember whether you were there — really there, not perfect, just present.

So here's your permission slip: lower the bar. Be good enough. It's more than enough.
